# Game Requirements

## Technical Requirements

- **REQ-01:** Build a small Canvas game using Vite and TypeScript.
- **REQ-19:** Maintain automated test coverage for every documented requirement.

## Domain Rules

- **REQ-02:** Simulate spaceship and asteroid physics.
- **REQ-06:** Turn the ship directly toward the current mouse or touch position.
- **REQ-07:** While a touch pointer is pressed, accelerate the ship toward that position. The forward component of its existing velocity is preserved; the perpendicular and reverse components are dampened per REQ-26. (On PC the mouse only aims; thrust is via WASD per REQ-37.)
- **REQ-08:** Scale touch thrust with the distance between the ship and the pointer: nearby input produces little thrust, while distant input produces strong thrust.
- **REQ-11:** Populate the world with randomized asteroids, weighted toward small asteroids.
- **REQ-12:** Give the ship and asteroids area-scaled mass based on their radius, and use their relative masses during physical collisions to determine their resulting directions and speeds while transferring momentum and asteroid spin. Drones, pirates, and regular asteroids also collide physically with one another, transferring momentum and spin; high-speed impacts whose impact speed exceeds 500 damage drones and pirates, and crash-destroy them when their hit-points are depleted (emitting a destruction burst like an exploding ship).
- **REQ-14:** Randomly and stably spread collectible fuel, repair, and ammo containers throughout reached world regions rather than only around the starting center.
- **REQ-16:** Reduce the ship's fuel while it is thrusting.
- **REQ-18:** Allow supply containers to collide physically with asteroids.
- **REQ-20:** Include immovable massive asteroids that are 30–100 times the ship radius, have irregular concave silhouettes, and physically collide with the ship, regular asteroids, and supply containers. Their impact craters must not overlap the outline, must span varied sizes with more small craters than large ones, and must not be limited to the asteroid center.
- **REQ-21:** Match massive-asteroid collisions to their irregular visible outline so concave areas do not create invisible collisions outside the rock.
- **REQ-22:** Treat the ship as a swept sphere: expand polygon edges by the ship radius, cap original edge corners with ship-radius circles, find the earliest collision along the previous-to-current center path, keep the ship outside the obstacle, and handle simultaneous contacts without penetration.
- **REQ-24:** Stably spread massive asteroids through distant world regions rather than only around the starting center.
- **REQ-25:** Spawn newly recycled or region-generated world objects beyond the visible boundary, with additional clearance as ship speed increases.
- **REQ-26:** While the ship is thrusting, dampen the perpendicular and reverse components of its velocity at a tunable per-second rate, so motion across or against the thrust direction bleeds off while forward motion is retained.
- **REQ-33:** Give the ship a hit-points meter alongside fuel, and display it with them. Violent collisions—those whose impact speed exceeds 500—damage the ship. Collisions with massive asteroids inflict more damage than collisions with regular asteroids. The maximum damage a single collision can inflict is tuned so the ship just barely survives two full-speed impacts.
- **REQ-34:** Randomly and stably spread collectible repair containers throughout reached world regions—like the other supply containers—that restore the ship's hit-points when collected.
- **REQ-35:** After the ship takes damage, grant it 0.5 seconds of invulnerability so repeated contacts cannot drain hit-points instantly, and emit a large burst of particles at the impact point.
- **REQ-36:** When the ship's hit-points reach zero, destroy it with an explosion, show a Game Over screen, and start a new game when the player clicks.
- **REQ-37:** On PC, give the player directional thrust via the WASD keys: W thrusts forward along the ship's nose, S backward, A left, and D right. The keys combine into a single ship-axis thrust vector whose magnitude ramps up while held and decays when released, so thrust tapers smoothly on key release. The perpendicular and reverse velocity components are dampened relative to the active thrust direction per REQ-26, and fuel is consumed proportionally to the thrust level. The default maximum speed is 1000.
- **REQ-38:** Show visible thruster nozzles — tail, nose, port, and starboard — that emit fuel-amber exhaust particles (cooling to atmospheric dust per REQ-10/31) when their corresponding thrust direction is active.
- **REQ-39:** Fire red laser shots forward from the ship's nose as traveling projectiles when the player holds the left mouse button or the space key, auto-firing at a fixed rate and consuming ammo per shot.
- **REQ-40:** Give the ship a depletable ammo meter displayed in the HUD with fuel and hit-points, consumed by firing and replenished by collecting ammo containers, which are stably spread through reached world regions like the other supply containers.
- **REQ-41:** Make laser impacts affect regular asteroids by size tier: large asteroids may split into two medium, medium into two small, and the smallest explode and may leave a random supply collectable. Massive asteroids are unaffected and only emit particles.
- **REQ-42:** When the ship's ammo or fuel reaches zero, passively regenerate one unit every two seconds so the ship is never permanently stranded.
- **REQ-43:** Resolve laser impacts against massive asteroids using their irregular visible outline rather than the bounding circle, so shots passing through concave areas are not absorbed.
- **REQ-44:** Despawn laser shots once they leave the visible screen.
- **REQ-45:** Play sound effects for in-game events, selecting each event's variation from its dedicated `assets/sounds` folder: a laser-shot sound on firing, a laser-impact sound when a shot strikes an asteroid, an asteroid-collision sound when two asteroids collide, a ship-collision sound when the ship is damaged by an asteroid, and a collectable-pickup sound when the ship collects a supply container. The thrust sound is divided into start, loop, and end segments of its single file: the start segment plays when thrust begins, the loop segment repeats while thrusting, and the end segment plays when thrust stops. Hunting mining drones play the same thrust sound at a lower volume, started and stopped independently of the ship's thrust while a hunting drone is on-screen. Hunting space pirates (per REQ-63) likewise play an engine thrust sound at a lower volume than the ship, started and stopped independently while a hunting pirate is on-screen; pirates play a laser-shot sound at a lower volume when they fire, and a collision sound at a lower volume for their non-ship collisions (pirate-asteroid collisions). Pirate-ship collisions use the ship-collision sound like any ship damage. Only play sound effects for events occurring within the current on-screen view; events off-screen remain silent. Provide a master sound-effects volume slider plus an individual volume slider for each effect type in the settings menu, persisted across reloads through the storage adapter and restored on startup, and stay silent until the first user interaction unlocks audio playback. The master sound-effects volume slider is a user setting; the individual per-effect-type volume sliders are dev settings shown only during development builds (per REQ-78).
- **REQ-47:** When the ship comes within three ship lengths (six times the ship radius) of a collectible supply container, attract the container toward the ship so it is easy to pick up, pulling faster the closer it gets.
- **REQ-48:** Mining drones attach themselves to asteroids of every size — small, medium, and large regular asteroids as well as massive asteroids — gripping the host surface. When the ship comes within range the drone detaches and accelerates toward the ship, damaging it on impact and self-destructing in the process (the ship's 0.5-second damage invulnerability per REQ-35 still applies). Firing the laser on the same screen as an attached drone also awakens it. A drone that is hit by a laser and survives instantly detaches and becomes a hunter. Hunting drones thrust toward the ship and emit fuel-amber exhaust particles (per REQ-10) behind them. Drones survive one to three laser hits before being destroyed. Drones are spawned attached to asteroids beyond the visible boundary (per REQ-25) and recycled when they drift far beyond it; if a host is destroyed or recycled, the drone releases and becomes a hunter.
- **REQ-51:** When spawning a supply container — whether generated in a newly reached world region or dropped from a destroyed asteroid — favor the resource whose ship meter is lowest, unless a container of that type is already visible on screen, in which case favor the next-lowest unrepresented resource; when every type is already visible, choose randomly. Ties for the lowest meter are broken randomly.
- **REQ-52:** Run a first mission: start the ship at 50% fuel, hull, and ammo; display "Mission: refill resources, watch out for mining drones. Click to continue." and wait for the player's click before the simulation begins.
- **REQ-53:** End the first mission when the ship's fuel exceeds 90, hull is full, ammo exceeds 90, and no mining drones are hunting; display "well done" with "We have picked up a long distance signal." and wait for the player's click before continuing.
- **REQ-54:** After the first mission, when the player flies into space they have not previously visited, stop spawning new world objects there so the area is empty; entering such uncharted space — and being at least one visible-screen radius away from any regular or massive asteroid — starts the second mission.
- **REQ-55:** Display "Mission 2: Traverse empty space towards signal." with "Destroy pirates to recover both wing-gun upgrades." and "click to continue" for the second mission and wait for the player's click; on click, begin the traversal mission rather than restarting.
- **REQ-56:** When the first mission ends, randomize a signal direction. Indicate it with a dotted red line drawn inward from the screen edge on the signal side, plus a growing-arc radio-wave indicator (red, concentric circular arcs centered at the same screen-edge anchor, expanding inward and fading over time), and show the same direction on the minimap as a dotted red line from the map center toward the map edge plus a matching growing-arc wave at the map edge.
- **REQ-57:** Show a blue growing-arc directional wave — same style as the signal wave — on the screen edge toward each off-screen hunting mining drone, so the player can sense where hunters are coming from. These blue drone waves are not shown on the minimap.
- **REQ-60:** During the second mission's traversal, zoom the camera and the minimap farther out to convey desolation and empty space. The camera's desolation zoom composes with the speed zoom (REQ-09), the resting zoom (REQ-50), and the enemy-threat pullback (REQ-56), and the minimap enlarges its world span so a wider area is visible.
- **REQ-61:** While traversing the second mission, show a distance meter indicating how far the ship still has to travel toward the signal source (approximately three minutes of flying time at cruise speed), expressed as a remaining distance.
- **REQ-62:** While traversing the second mission, keep space mostly empty but periodically spawn dense islands of asteroids along the route that can be mined for resources. Islands vary in size (10–100 rocks) and irregular, non-round shape, with wide randomized spacing between them so long empty stretches separate encounters; some islands spawn peripherally (off to the side of the route) so they can be seen and skipped without interaction. Show a faint warning outline matching each island's irregular silhouette (like the drone listening radius) so the player can spot it from afar. Suppress ambient supply-container spreading during the second mission so resources come only from mining asteroid drops.
- **REQ-63:** While traversing the second mission, spawn space pirate ships — larger than mining drones and ship-like in their construction — that begin appearing after the ship has traveled roughly one quarter of the route. They spawn in unexplored area so they remain undiscovered until the ship explores into them, and their detection ring is hidden until they awaken, and they awaken when the ship comes within a larger detection distance and start hunting it using airplane-like pursuit: pirates bank and turn at a limited rate (they cannot snap to face the ship), thrust forward along their nose rather than beelining, and when close they turn away and loop back for another pass rather than closing head-on. Pirates steer away to avoid colliding with the ship at close range. Pirate ships fly faster than the ship (which defaults to a maximum speed of 1000) so they can keep up and must be dealt with, and they fire lasers at the ship only when their nose is roughly aimed at it. Pirate laser shots destroy (and split) regular asteroids and spark off massive asteroids, and can also hit and destroy other pirate ships (friendly fire). When a pirate and the ship collide, both bounce apart; if the impact speed exceeds the violent threshold (per REQ-33), both take damage — the ship scaled like a regular-asteroid hit (per REQ-33) and the pirate one hit-point (per REQ-12) — and a pirate reduced to zero hit-points crashes and is destroyed like an exploding ship. The ship's 0.5-second damage invulnerability (per REQ-35) still applies. Pirate ships spawn in grouped squads of two to three around shared centers; combat squads spawn ahead along the route so the ship encounters them as they travel, and peripheral squads spawn off-route and are discovered only if the ship explores off-route. Pirate pursuit triggers action music. Hunting pirates emit fuel-amber exhaust particles (per REQ-10) behind them, like mining drones.
- **REQ-64:** End the second mission when the remaining distance is covered and the ship has recovered both wing-gun upgrades; reveal a huge abandoned rusty maze space station (per REQ-79) at the destination — arrival is reaching its entrance — and display "Well done" with "Click to continue.", then wait for the player's click. The station is stationary and its hull collides like a huge asteroid, but mining drones do not attach or re-home to it.
- **REQ-65:** After the second mission ends and the player clicks to continue, proceed to the Mission 3 briefing without restarting the game, preserving the destination space station.
- **REQ-66:** Provide a missions menu, always available, that lets the player swap to a chosen mission's briefing by fully restarting the game at that mission. Selecting Mission 2 starts the ship with full resources and lands on the Mission 2 briefing. Include a Mission 3 cheat entry that starts the ship with full resources and both wing guns beside the destination space station on the Mission 3 briefing.
- **REQ-67:** During the second mission, render beautiful gas-cloud nebulas made of large, visible particles that wisp out of the way when the ship drives through them and spring back afterward. Clouds are very dense, packing far more particles into each cloud, and the ship, pirates, and mining drones strongly part them so each leaves a visible trail. Nebulas are independent of the particle-visibility setting. Nebulas are large (triple the area of a baseline cloud), appear rarely with wide randomized spacing, vary in irregular shape (elongated ellipses, multi-lobed blobs, and wispy tendrils), and some clouds spawn peripherally off the route so they frame the journey without blocking it.
- **REQ-68:** When a pirate ship is destroyed, it has a 20% chance of dropping a weapon pod collectible, and an independent 35% chance of dropping a fuel, repair, or ammo supply container (chosen by lowest ship meter like an asteroid drop, per REQ-51) so pirates serve as a resource source during the empty-space traversal.
- **REQ-69:** Collecting a weapon pod upgrades the ship's laser in two steps: the first pod adds a forward gun on the left wing, the second adds one on the right wing, so a fully upgraded ship fires three laser shots forward per volley. The wing guns are visible on the ship's wings.
- **REQ-70:** When the ship's speed exceeds the damage threshold (per REQ-33's violent impact speed), show the HUD speed readout in red with a "WARNING" label above it so the player can see they are going fast enough to take collision damage.
- **REQ-71:** Freshly spawned explosion, damage-burst, and collision particles (per REQ-13/35/36) burn brighter and for longer before cooling to atmospheric dust, so bursts are visibly intense rather than vanishing quickly into the atmospheric field. Exhaust particles (per REQ-10/38) are unchanged.
- **REQ-72:** When a mining drone is destroyed (per REQ-48), it has a 35% chance of dropping a fuel, repair, or ammo supply container chosen by lowest ship meter like an asteroid drop (per REQ-51), so drones serve as an additional resource source. No weapon pods are dropped.
- **REQ-73:** During the second mission's traversal, some asteroid islands (per REQ-62) — roughly 30% of them — carry 1 to 8 mining drones attached to the island's asteroids. These drones behave like mission-1 drones: they detach and hunt the ship when it comes within range (per REQ-48) and are recycled when they drift far beyond the visible boundary.
- **REQ-75:** During the second mission's traversal, do not spawn new asteroid islands, island mining drones, pirate squads, or nebulas within the beginning 10% or ending 10% of the travel distance. Encounters that already exist continue to update normally.
- **REQ-76:** Display "Mission 3: Enter the abandoned space station and reach its central chamber." with "Find the switches to open the gates. Click to continue." for Mission 3. After continuing, Mission 3 takes place inside the abandoned maze space station (per REQ-79): the ship enters through the station entrance and must navigate the maze, opening three gates (per REQ-80) to reach the central chamber. Reaching the central chamber completes the mission with a "Well done" screen and waits for the player's click; there is no Mission 4.
- **REQ-82:** While the ship's speed is below 100, thrust consumes no fuel, and the exhaust particles use a cooler color to indicate that no thrust is being spent.
- **REQ-83:** When the ship's speed is below 30 and no thrust has been applied for two seconds, automatically brake the ship to a full stop.
- **REQ-84:** When the ship reaches its top speed, thrust consumes no fuel unless it is used to correct the ship's direction — only the non-forward component of thrust relative to the ship's velocity costs fuel. While thrust is free at top speed, the exhaust particles use a cooler color.

## View Requirements

- **REQ-03:** Keep the player ship within the middle third of the screen, shifted opposite its travel direction so the player sees more space ahead where they are heading. The offset scales with the ship's velocity, transitions smoothly, and the ship rests centered on screen when not moving.
- **REQ-04:** Render the ship as a simple triangle.
- **REQ-05:** Support mouse, touch, and keyboard controls.
- **REQ-09:** Zoom the camera farther out as the ship moves faster.
- **REQ-10:** Emit world-space exhaust particles behind the ship, opposite its thrust direction. They carry a world-space velocity so they drift through the world rather than sitting still. They are born into the atmospheric particle field (per REQ-31) with a fuel-amber initial color that cools to atmospheric dust over a brief bright burn, rather than vanishing, and are removed when they leave the visible range like all atmospheric particles.
- **REQ-13:** Display visual particles when collisions occur. They are born into the atmospheric particle field (per REQ-31) with an impact-tinted initial color that cools to atmospheric dust, and persist as atmospheric particles rather than vanishing.
- **REQ-15:** Display UI indicators showing the ship's fuel level.
- **REQ-23:** Display a ship-centered scrolling minimap of camera-explored space, discovered regular asteroids, discovered fuel, repair, and ammo containers, discovered mining drones, discovered pirate ships, and discovered massive asteroids represented by their irregular outlines.
- **REQ-81:** Show the abandoned space station (per REQ-79) on the minimap — its hull, interior walls, gates, switches, central chamber, and collectible supply containers — once it has been placed.
- **REQ-82:** Station walls are squared polygon plates derived from the bitmap boundary vectors (per REQ-79): each wall is a rectangle built from a traced boundary segment, with no rounded vertex spheres. The ship collides as a swept sphere against the wall polygon (per REQ-22), so corners where walls share an endpoint seal without gaps and the ship does not get trapped in sphere pockets. Regular asteroids, supply containers, and collectibles collide with wall polygons via radial-outline push-out. The ship cannot slip through.
- **REQ-27:** Provide a settings menu with sliders to tune the ship's lateral dampening rate, thrust power, and maximum speed at runtime.
- **REQ-28:** Persist the chosen control-tuning settings across page reloads through a storage adapter that abstracts where and how they are stored, and restore them when the settings menu initializes.
- **REQ-29:** Play adaptive background music divided into calm, medium, and action categories. Calm plays while the player is just piloting and idle; medium takes over after at least three laser shots fall within a single five-second window and a five-second dwell elapses; action starts and ends immediately when an enemy is pursuing (overriding medium without waiting for the dwell). When the firing window empties, music returns to calm after the dwell. Category switches crossfade, tracks rotate to the next song when the current one ends, and a paused track resumes from its remembered position. The music level fades to silence when the slider is at zero, and the system stays silent until the first user interaction unlocks audio playback. Each mission has one calm theme song — mission 1's theme is song 1, mission 2's is song 2, mission 3's is song 3 — named for their missions in `assets/music`. When a mission starts, its theme song plays from the beginning, and only the theme songs of missions reached so far are unlocked for the calm rotation: mission 1 plays only song 1; mission 2 starts with song 2 then rotates through songs 1 and 2; mission 3 starts with song 3 then rotates through songs 1, 2, and 3, repeating in that order.
- **REQ-31:** Maintain a single atmospheric particle field drifting through model space at random. Particles are spawned at random outside the visible range with a random speed directed inward into view, bounce visually off the ship, regular asteroids, and massive asteroids (bounding-circle broad phase, then outline-accurate against the irregular silhouette), are removed when they leave the visible range, and are replenished up to a target population counting all particles regardless of origin. Exhaust (REQ-10/38), collision (REQ-13), damage-burst (REQ-35), and explosion (REQ-36/41) particles are born into this same field with hotter initial colors that cool to atmospheric dust; they are not singled out beyond initial color and are kept even when their color cools and visibility is reduced.
- **REQ-32:** Provide a persistent particle-visibility setting in the settings menu (a slider controlling the opacity of exhaust, collision, and atmospheric-dust particles), restored on load and saved on change through the storage adapter. This particle-visibility slider is a dev setting, shown only during development builds (per REQ-78).
- **REQ-49:** Render mining drones as a blue, irregular body with gripping arms on one side; the arms face the host asteroid while attached and the ship while hunting.
- **REQ-50:** Provide a persistent default zoom-in level slider in the settings menu that sets the camera's resting zoom (the zoom applied when the ship is idle), restored on load and saved on change through the storage adapter. The camera still zooms farther out as the ship moves faster per REQ-09.
- **REQ-56:** While any mining drone or space pirate is hunting, zoom the camera farther out so the player can react to attacking enemies more easily. The pullback composes with the speed zoom (REQ-09) and the resting zoom (REQ-50), and eases smoothly in and out as the threat begins and ends.
- **REQ-57:** While a mining drone is attached and inactive, render a thin ring around it at its detection radius (the distance at which the ship awakens it per REQ-48) so the player can see and avoid waking it.
- **REQ-58:** A hunting drone that the ship outruns beyond a fixed give-up distance stops hunting and flies to the nearest asteroid to re-attach, so hunters can be lost by outrunning them. Re-homing drones are no longer considered hunting.
- **REQ-59:** While any mining drone is hunting, render a static faint ring around the ship at the give-up distance (per REQ-58) so the player can see how far they must pull ahead to escape.
- **REQ-70:** A star shines fixed directional world-space light onto regular asteroids, massive asteroids, the ship, mining drones, pirate ships, and supply/weapon pods, giving each a lit side and a dark side. The lit side is markedly brighter than the dark side with high contrast, brightest at the rim facing the star (near-white), in the style of high-contrast space cinema. Dark and shadowed sides remain visible rather than disappearing to pure black.
- **REQ-71:** All asteroids cast directional shadows whose length scales with the asteroid's size (small asteroids cast short shadows, massive ones cast long shadows). Shadows are rendered as a visible shadow volume on an offscreen shadow map that darkens space and the starfield where it falls via a multiply composite, so the shadow cone can be seen. Smaller objects and atmospheric particles within a shadow darken on all sides (half-shaded near the cone edges) and still remain visible.
- **REQ-74:** Show the current mission's goals as a UI checklist centered at the bottom of the screen, with a checkbox for each goal that becomes checked as the player meets it. Mission 1 lists refilling fuel above 90%, refilling hull to full, refilling ammo above 90%, and avoiding getting killed by mining drones; mission 2 lists traversing to the signal, recovering the left wing-gun upgrade, and recovering the right wing-gun upgrade, with the wing-gun goals hinting that pirates must be destroyed to recover them; mission 3 lists opening gate 1, opening gate 2, opening gate 3, and reaching the central chamber.
- **REQ-77:** While the ship is in the post-hit invulnerable state (per REQ-35), render a circle around the ship so the player can see it is currently invulnerable.
- **REQ-78:** Divide the settings menu into user settings and dev settings. User settings — lateral dampening, thrust power, maximum speed, music level, default zoom, and the master sound-effects volume — are shown in all builds. Dev settings — particle visibility and the individual per-effect-type sound volumes — are shown only during development builds and hidden in production builds; their persisted or default values still apply in production.
- **REQ-79:** The abandoned space station — the mission 2 destination that persists into mission 3 (per REQ-65) — is a maze-scale stationary structure (outer radius on the order of 1000 units). It is built by carving rooms and corridors out of a solid bitmap, then tracing the boundary vectors of the carved voids to form walls — the outer hull is built around the interior rather than placed as blocks. It has a single flyable entrance on its exterior (the mission 2 arrival target, where the entrance room carves through the hull) and an interior randomized maze of corridors, docking bays, abandoned machinery (decorative immovable collidable pillars), collectible supply containers, and a larger open central chamber at its center. Its walls are immovable and collide with the ship, regular asteroids, and supply containers (per REQ-82); mining drones do not attach to or re-home on it. The station replaces ambient massive asteroids at the destination.
- **REQ-80:** Three gates block progress through the station interior, each opened permanently by a corresponding switch located in the area before it. A switch activates when any physics body — the ship, a regular asteroid, a mining drone, a pirate, or a laser shot — collides with it. The maze is randomized per playthrough but constrained so that, in order: switch 1 is reachable from the entrance without gate 1 being open, and gate 1 is encountered before switch 1; switch 2 is reachable only after gate 1 is open, and gate 2 is encountered before switch 2; switch 3 is reachable only after gate 2 is open, and gate 3 is encountered before switch 3. Gate 3 leads into the central chamber; reaching the central chamber completes mission 3.
